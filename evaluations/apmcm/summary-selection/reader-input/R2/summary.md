## Summary
For the normalized benefit criteria (higher is better), the score is S = w·criterion1 + (1−w)·criterion2, with S_X = 3 + 6w and S_Y = 8 − 2w. At w = 0.4 the scores are X 5.4, Y 7.2 and Z 3.2, so Y is the unique winner. As a function of w, Y wins for 0 ≤ w < 5/8, X and Y tie at w = 5/8, and X wins for 5/8 < w ≤ 1; Z is strictly dominated by X and never wins. When X's first criterion independently varies in [8, 10] and Y's in [5, 7], with all other entries fixed, S_X − S_Y ranges over [6w − 5, 10w − 5]. Y is guaranteed no worse than every competitor for 0 ≤ w ≤ 1/2 and X for 5/6 ≤ w ≤ 1; at the boundary an extreme perturbation can force a tie, so both are weak guarantees. For 1/2 < w < 5/6 the winner depends on the realized criterion values, so only a conditional choice is justified, and Z remains dominated throughout. These are exact analytic regions over a bounded uncertainty set, not probabilities, confidence intervals, or reliability claims.
