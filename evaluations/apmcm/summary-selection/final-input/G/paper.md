# Identifying a cooling model through complementary synthetic experiments

## Summary
We study a lumped cooling model with ambient temperature Ta = 20 degrees C, initial temperature T0 = 30 degrees C, thermal conductance h measured in W/K, and thermal capacitance C measured in Wh/K. Time is in hours. The model is C dT/dt = -h(T-Ta), so T(t)=20+10 exp(-kt), where k=h/C. The supplied transient observations are at times 0, 1, 2 and 4 hours, with temperatures 30, 27.78800783071405, 26.065306597126334 and 23.678794411714424 degrees C. A zero-intercept log-linear calculation gives k=0.25 per hour. The transient observations alone do not distinguish h from C because scaling them together leaves k and every predicted temperature unchanged; examples h=2.5,C=10 and h=5,C=20 give exactly the same transient. A separate steady-heating experiment uses 50 W and a 10 K equilibrium rise, hence h=50/10=5 W/K and C=h/k=20 Wh/K, equal to 72000 J/K. These are synthetic identifications and not physical parameter measurements. For cooling from 30 to 24 degrees C at 20 degrees C ambient, the fixed exponential model gives t=ln(10/4)/0.25=3.66516292749662 hours, rounded to 3.67 hours. Therefore the 3-hour requirement is not met. A first-order tangent extrapolation at the initial cooling rate -2.5 K/h would instead give 2.4 hours, which is an inappropriate deadline decision because the cooling rate decreases as temperature approaches ambient. The joint experiment supplies the missing independent scale information, and the exact transient equation gives the correct within-model deadline assessment. Synthetic data, exact model generation and absent measurement uncertainty do not establish physical accuracy, a confidence interval or real-device performance.

## Model and data
We assume fixed ambient temperature, constant positive h and C, no additional heat source during cooling, and a lumped uniform temperature. Conductance times temperature difference has units W; C in Wh/K times a rate in K/h also has units W. The solution is T(t)=Ta+(T0-Ta)exp[-(h/C)t]. The transient values listed above were generated exactly from k=0.25 per hour, not measured from equipment. The machine data retain floating-point precision. The heating experiment is a separate synthetic steady-state condition; at equilibrium input power equals h times the temperature rise.

## Q1: identifiable rate, not separate parameters
For t>0 define y=ln[(T-Ta)/(T0-Ta)], with y=-kt. A zero-intercept least-squares fit gives k=0.25 per hour. Only h/C is identifiable from this transient: any simultaneous positive rescaling of h and C produces the same curve. Both (h,C)=(2.5 W/K,10 Wh/K) and (5 W/K,20 Wh/K) fit identically. A unique conductance or capacitance cannot be reported from Q1 alone. Zero residual on data generated by the model is not independent physical validation.

## Q2: independent scale information
The separate steady state uses 50 W and a 10 K equilibrium rise. Therefore h=50/10=5 W/K. Combining it with k gives C=h/k=20 Wh/K, equivalent to 72000 J/K. The separate synthetic condition resolves the scale ambiguity under the assumed model; the two parameters are not identified from the transient alone. No confidence interval is available because no measurement-error model or measurement data are provided.

## Q3: deadline decision
Solving 24=20+10exp(-0.25t) yields t=ln(2.5)/0.25=3.66516292749662 h, reported as 3.67 h. This exceeds 3 h, so the specified cooling condition fails the deadline. The initial tangent has slope -k(T0-Ta)=-2.5 K/h and would predict (30-24)/2.5=2.4 h; this ignores the declining cooling rate and reverses the deadline decision. The exact exponential model, not tangent extrapolation, is used for the recommendation.

## Conclusion and limitations
Use the transient to identify k, combine the separate steady-state condition to identify h and C, and apply the exact cooling law to assess the deadline. The supplied model predicts failure of the 3-hour requirement. These claims are conditional on the stipulated lumped model and synthetic inputs. Unknown heat losses, spatial variation, measurement noise, parameter variability and real-device performance remain unvalidated; none is silently added to the data.
