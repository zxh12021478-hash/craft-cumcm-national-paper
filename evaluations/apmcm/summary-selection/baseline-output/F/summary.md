## Summary

We select among five fixed parcel-dispatch policies (A–E) on two 100-request batches, where a policy is admissible only if it serves at least 90% of requests and its maximum delivery time over served requests is at most 6 hours; among admissible policies we minimize complete operating cost (CNY). On the base batch, four policies (A, B, D, E) meet both constraints; C is cheaper but excluded because its maximum delivery time is 7.0 h. Policy B is the least-cost admissible choice at CNY 210, serving 95% of requests with a 5.0 h maximum, versus reference policy A at CNY 240 — a 12.5% cost reduction. On the stress batch, D is the only admissible policy (CNY 255, 94% served, 5.7 h maximum): B fails the service floor at 84% and E fails the time bound at 6.5 h. We therefore recommend B for the base batch and D for the stress batch. Because no distribution links the batches, the recommendations are scenario-qualified rather than unconditional. The results come solely from the supplied synthetic tables via exhaustive constraint screening: they do not show that B is optimal among all possible dispatch policies, predict performance on future batches, or constitute empirical deployment validation, and no confidence intervals or reliability probabilities are asserted.
