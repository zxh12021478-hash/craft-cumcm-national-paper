## Summary

We identify the parameters of the lumped cooling model C dT/dt = −h(T − Ta) (time in hours, h in W/K, C in Wh/K) from synthetic experiments and assess a cooling deadline. A zero-intercept log-linear fit of the transient observations (T0 = 30 °C, Ta = 20 °C) identifies only the rate k = h/C = 0.25 h⁻¹: the transient alone cannot separate h from C, since any common positive rescaling of both parameters leaves every predicted temperature unchanged (e.g. (h, C) = (2.5 W/K, 10 Wh/K) and (5 W/K, 20 Wh/K) fit identically). A separate steady-heating experiment at 50 W with a 10 K equilibrium rise supplies the missing scale information, giving h = 50/10 = 5 W/K and hence C = h/k = 20 Wh/K (72,000 J/K). Under the fixed model, cooling from 30 to 24 °C at 20 °C ambient takes t = ln(10/4)/0.25 = 3.67 h, so the 3-hour requirement is not met. A first-order tangent extrapolation at the initial cooling rate (−2.5 K/h) would give 2.4 h and reverse the decision, but it ignores the declining cooling rate as the temperature approaches ambient; the exact exponential law is used instead. All inputs are synthetic and generated exactly from the stipulated model, with no measurement uncertainty provided, so the results are within-model identifications — not physical parameter measurements, confidence intervals, or evidence of real-device performance.
