## Summary
For the lumped cooling model C dT/dt = −h(T − Ta), with time in hours, the solution is T(t) = Ta + (T0 − Ta) exp(−kt), where k = h/C. The transient observations give k = 0.25 h⁻¹, but the transient alone identifies only this ratio: any common positive rescaling of h and C leaves k and the predicted temperatures unchanged, so h and C cannot be separated. The separate steady-heating experiment (50 W input, 10 K equilibrium rise) supplies the missing scale, giving h = 5 W/K and C = 20 Wh/K. Under the fixed model, cooling from 30 °C to 24 °C at a 20 °C ambient takes 3.67 h, so the 3-hour requirement is not met; a first-order tangent at the initial cooling rate would give 2.4 h and reverse the decision by ignoring the declining rate as temperature approaches ambient. These are exact results for the stipulated lumped model with synthetic data and no supplied measurement-error model; they are not physical parameter measurements, confidence intervals, or evidence of real-device performance.
