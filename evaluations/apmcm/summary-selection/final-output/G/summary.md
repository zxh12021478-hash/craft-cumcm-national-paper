## Summary
For the lumped cooling model C dT/dt = −h(T − Ta), with time in hours, the solution is T(t) = Ta + (T0 − Ta) exp(−kt), where k = h/C. A zero-intercept fit to the transient observations gives k = 0.25 h⁻¹, but the transient alone identifies only this ratio: any common positive rescaling of h and C leaves k and every predicted temperature unchanged, so h and C cannot be separated from Q1. The separate steady-heating experiment (50 W input, 10 K equilibrium rise) supplies the missing scale: h = 50/10 = 5 W/K and then C = h/k = 20 Wh/K (72,000 J/K). Under the fixed model, cooling from 30 °C to 24 °C at a 20 °C ambient takes t = ln(10/4)/0.25 = 3.67 h, so the 3-hour requirement is not met; a first-order tangent at the initial cooling rate would give 2.4 h and reverse the decision by ignoring the declining rate as temperature approaches ambient. These are exact results for the stipulated lumped model with synthetic data and no measurement uncertainty; they are not physical parameter measurements, confidence intervals, or evidence of real-device performance.
