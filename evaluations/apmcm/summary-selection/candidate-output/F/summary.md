## Summary
We screen five fixed dispatch policies on each of two 100-request batches, requiring at least 90% of requests served and a maximum delivery time of at most 6 hours among served requests, and then choose the cheapest feasible policy; low cost cannot compensate a failed constraint. On the base batch, A, B, D and E satisfy both requirements, while C is the cheapest policy overall at CNY 205 but violates the time limit at 7.0 hours. The least-cost feasible policy is therefore B, at CNY 210 with 95% served and a 5.0-hour maximum, which is 12.5% below the defined reference A at CNY 240. On the stress batch, D is the only feasible policy: A, B and C serve only 88%, 84% and 87%, and E passes the service floor at 97% but exceeds the time limit at 6.5 hours; D costs CNY 255 with 94% served and a 5.7-hour maximum. We accordingly recommend B for the base batch and D for the stress batch. This recommendation is scenario-qualified and rests on exhaustive comparison over the five supplied policies alone: the records are synthetic observations with no distribution linking the batches, and they establish neither optimality over other dispatch policies nor empirical deployment performance.
