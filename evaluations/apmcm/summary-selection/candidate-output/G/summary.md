## Summary
For the lumped cooling model C dT/dt = -h(T - Ta) with Ta = 20 °C, the solution is T(t) = 20 + 10 exp(-kt) with k = h/C. A zero-intercept log-linear fit to the transient observations gives k = 0.25 per hour, but the transient alone cannot separate h and C: any common positive rescaling of the two parameters leaves k and every predicted temperature unchanged, so Q1 identifies only the rate ratio. The separate steady-heating experiment—50 W input at a 10 K equilibrium rise—supplies the missing scale through h = P/ΔT, giving h = 5 W/K and then C = h/k = 20 Wh/K (72,000 J/K). Under the fixed model, cooling from 30 to 24 °C takes t = ln(10/4)/0.25 = 3.67 hours, so the 3-hour requirement is not met; a first-order tangent at the initial cooling rate would instead give 2.4 hours and reverse the decision by ignoring the decline of the cooling rate as the body approaches ambient. All values are exact consequences of the stipulated synthetic model: no measurement uncertainty is supplied, and no physical accuracy or real-device performance is claimed.
