# Summary

We compare 384 enclosure and operating configurations for a three-in-one air conditioner, particle purifier and humidifier using a conservative 24-compartment model. Directional supply and intake kernels, closed by a volume-balanced return circulation, connect port settings to heat, particle and moisture transport; shell area controls assumed fan pressure and evaporation capacity.

The separate thermal, particle and humidity objectives select different configurations. All selected devices are floor mounted at the middle of the short wall, with a low single inlet, 600 m3/h flow and outlets angled 25 degrees upward. For Q1, a cylinder 0.360 m in diameter and 0.884 m high with one outlet minimizes the season-average temperature MAE at 3.501 C. For Q2, a 0.300 x 0.300 x 1.000 m tower with two outlets minimizes normalized one-hour particle exposure at 0.210. For Q3, the same tower with one outlet minimizes normalized humidity error at 0.167; winter mean relative humidity reaches 42.76% after one hour, below the 50% target.

For Q4, that single-outlet tower minimizes the equal-weight normalized composite objective at 0.376398. Its objective is 8.947% below the declared corner-tower baseline at 400 m3/h and 3.195% below the baseline at the same 600 m3/h flow. The same-flow gain compares placement and outlet angle within the same tower shell. One-hour electrical energy is 1.627 kWh in winter and 1.165 kWh in summer; the objective reductions are not energy-saving percentages.

The selected integrated design remains best after all 384 candidates are reranked with background mixing 30% lower and higher. Halving the time step changes winter thermal MAE by 0.0055 C, while an analytic particle-removal limit exposes a 3.11% terminal Euler discrepancy. These checks support numerical consistency within the declared surrogate. Uncalibrated circulation kernels, fixed filtration efficiency, omitted gas removal and summer condensation, unverified component packing and assumed external heat rejection limit physical interpretation. The result is a reproducible finite-candidate design recommendation for validation, rather than certified device performance.
