# Summary

This paper studies forecast-informed inventory planning for an item with weekly demand: (i) comparing out-of-sample error of four-week-ahead demand forecasts, (ii) selecting an inventory strategy under capacity constraints, and (iii) stating how the recommendation changes under demand perturbations. The study is entirely synthetic. Demand and cost figures are simulated, monetary amounts are in CNY, and no deployment or physical energy measurement was performed.

For Q1, a regression model is compared with a seasonal-naive baseline on the same 20 nonoverlapping out-of-sample test targets at a four-week horizon, with training data and features restricted to information available at each forecast origin. Pooling squared errors over the common targets gives RMSE 8 items for regression versus 10 items for the seasonal-naive baseline, a 20% relative reduction. Regression is therefore recommended for the evaluated four-week forecasting task. This is an out-of-sample error comparison rather than a classification accuracy, and the fitted coefficients support no causal interpretation.

For Q2, the forecasts drive planned buffer requirements in a capacity-constrained inventory model that selects among 12 prespecified feasible candidates under equal service requirements, with candidate costs simulated on one common synthetic demand trajectory. Strategy P is the least-cost evaluated candidate at 120 CNY, compared with 150 CNY for seasonal-naive planning, a 20% cost reduction; all required capacity and stock-balance residual checks pass with tolerance 1e-8. P is optimal only within the evaluated candidate set; the model does not certify global optimality over unrestricted policies, and the selection is routine optimization rather than a new algorithm.

For Q3, under 20 prespecified demand perturbation scenarios spanning -10% to +10% with no assigned probabilities, P remains the least-cost evaluated candidate in 18 scenarios. Strategy R is preferable in the other two, both at +10% demand. The conditional recommendation is therefore P in the tested base setting, switching to R for the two tested +10% cases. Performance between or outside the tested scenarios is not established, and the 18-of-20 scenario frequency is not a win probability.

Overall, the evidence supports regression-informed planning with strategy P for the base case and strategy R as a contingency for the identified +10% demand cases. Because all figures are simulated costs on a synthetic dataset rather than measured field savings, external deployment validation is required before any real-world claim.
