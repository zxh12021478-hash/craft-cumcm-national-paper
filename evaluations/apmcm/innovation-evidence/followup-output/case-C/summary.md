## Summary

We choose a minimum-cost subset of three indivisible supply modules (A: 4 units/day for CNY 7; B: 3 units/day for CNY 5; C: 5 units/day for CNY 10), subject to supplying at least 7 units/day under normal operation and at least 3 units/day after the loss of any one installed module. Complete enumeration of all eight subsets yields exactly four feasible designs—A+B (CNY 12), B+C (CNY 15), A+C (CNY 17), and A+B+C (CNY 22)—and A+B is the unique minimum. For the selected design, B supplies 3 units/day if A fails and A supplies 4 units/day if B fails, so both stipulated single-module failures meet the required 3 units/day (Q1 and Q2). At CNY 12, A+B costs 20% less than the specified capacity-first reference B+C at CNY 15. These are exact results for the stipulated additive-capacity model and finite contingency set, obtained with integer enumeration and corroborated by an analytic cost lower bound; they make no claim to measured hardware reliability, failure probabilities, or performance under unspecified multiple failures.
