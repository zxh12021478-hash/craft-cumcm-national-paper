## Summary

We compare three storage-dispatch controllers on twenty synthetic scenarios (eight quiet, eight peak, four boundary), where an admissible policy must satisfy every hard constraint—including nonnegative terminal inventory—before complete operating cost is considered. The reference B and the retuned controller T are feasible on all twenty scenarios; T is the lowest-cost admissible choice on this set at 1,900 CNY versus 2,000 CNY for B (5%), and T is no worse than B on every scenario. Adding the look-ahead module M to T (policy TM) lowers cost on quiet scenarios (80 versus 95 CNY) but raises it on peak scenarios (130 versus 95 CNY) and is infeasible on all four boundary scenarios because terminal inventory becomes negative; TM's pass rate on the set is 80% (16/20), a coverage figure rather than a deployment probability. The evidence therefore supports T, not TM, and does not support attributing any gain to M: configurations were selected on the same scenarios used for the final comparison, TM's selection score discarded early-terminated runs, and no M-without-T variant or common tuning budget was evaluated. M's anticipation mechanism remains a hypothesis until the selection rule is corrected and the frozen policy is evaluated on independent, held-out scenarios.
