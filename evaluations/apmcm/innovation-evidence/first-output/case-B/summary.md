## Summary

We compare a reference dispatch policy P with a reserve-release refinement R on twelve held-out synthetic evaluation days, each containing 1,000 requests. Both policies were tuned over six configurations on thirty separate development days and frozen before evaluation; paired rows use identical days and inputs, and every cost covers the complete day under the same cost components. R cost CNY 11,400 in total versus CNY 12,000 for P, a 5% reduction, and was CNY 50 cheaper on each of the twelve days. R served 96.0% of requests (11,520 of 12,000) versus 94.0% for P (11,280 of 12,000), a gain of 2 percentage points; every daily service rate for both policies exceeded the required 90% floor, with a minimum of 94%, and all twenty-four runs were complete and satisfied every recorded hard constraint. All twelve paired daily comparisons favored R on cost. The twelve days, rather than the 12,000 requests, are the evaluation units, and no confidence interval, significance test, or probability of future feasibility is claimed. We therefore recommend R over P for the tested twelve-day set. This is a descriptive fixture comparison, not a global-optimality proof or a full causal attribution to the reserve-release mechanism; performance on unseen demand, real hardware, and equation fidelity of R's implementation remain unverified because no source implementation is available.
