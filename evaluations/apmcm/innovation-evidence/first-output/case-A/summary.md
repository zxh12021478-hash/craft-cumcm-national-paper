## Summary

We compare three storage-dispatch policies on twenty specified synthetic scenarios (eight quiet, eight peak, four boundary) under hard constraints of no demand drops and nonnegative terminal inventory, with complete operating cost in CNY as the objective. The reference controller B and the retuned controller T, which does not contain the adaptive look-ahead module M, completed and satisfied every recorded hard constraint on all twenty scenarios at total costs of CNY 2,000 and CNY 1,900 respectively; T is therefore 5% cheaper than B on this set. The combined policy TM, T with M, completed only sixteen of twenty scenarios and violated the terminal-inventory constraint on every boundary scenario, so it yields no valid dispatch plan there and no valid total cost. On the sixteen scenarios where all three policies completed, TM cost CNY 1,680 versus CNY 1,600 for B and CNY 1,520 for T; TM was 20% cheaper than B only within the quiet subset (CNY 640 vs 800) and 30% more expensive in the peak subset (CNY 1,040 vs 800). The previously reported 90% reliability is not supported by the run log: TM's hard-constraint pass rate is 16/20 = 80% while B and T pass at 100%. Because configurations were selected on the same twenty scenarios, TM had a ten-fold larger tuning budget, its selection score discarded terminated runs, and no M-on/M-off ablation was executed, the data cannot attribute any gain to module M or quantify deployment probability, since the scenario generator supplies no probability law. We therefore recommend T rather than TM for the specified scenario set and treat M as an unverified hypothesis. A recommendation beyond this set requires matched-budget retuning, a selection score that forbids incomplete runs, repaired feasibility on boundary scenarios, and a frozen held-out evaluation, none of which has been performed.
