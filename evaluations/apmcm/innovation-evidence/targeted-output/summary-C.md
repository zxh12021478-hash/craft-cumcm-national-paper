# Summary

We solve the minimum-cost modular supply problem: select a subset of three indivisible modules — A (capacity 4 units/day, installation cost CNY 7), B (3 units/day, CNY 5), and C (5 units/day, CNY 10), at most one of each — such that total supply under normal operation is at least 7 units/day and the supply remaining after the loss of any single installed module is at least 3 units/day. Capacities add without losses, and the stipulated normal and single-module-failure conditions form the entire finite contingency set.

Because the candidate set is finite (2^3 = 8 subsets), the provided solver enumerates every subset and recomputes normal supply, each single-failure residual, and cost with integer arithmetic, recording all eight rows rather than only successes. Exactly four designs are feasible: A+B (CNY 12, normal supply 7), B+C (CNY 15, 8), A+C (CNY 17, 9), and A+B+C (CNY 22, 12); the remaining four subsets fail the normal-supply requirement. The unique minimum-cost design is therefore **A+B at CNY 12** (Q1). Under its two stipulated single-module failures, remaining supply is **3 units/day if A fails** (B alone) and **4 units/day if B fails** (A alone); the worst specified post-failure supply equals the required 3 (Q2).

Compared with the specified reference design B+C at CNY 15 (normal supply 8; residuals 3 and 5 units/day after a single failure), the recommended design costs (15−12)/15 = **20% less** while meeting every stipulated condition. This is an exact complete-enumeration certificate for the stated additive-capacity model and the given finite contingency set, not merely a solver status. It establishes no hardware reliability, no failure probabilities, no performance under unspecified multiple failures, and no generalization beyond the stated conditions.
